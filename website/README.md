# Marjan Travel & Tours: website

A static site with no dependencies. The copy comes from `../Website-Content/*.docx`. The design draws on the Mobbin reference collection ("Marjan Travel – Website Inspiration").

## Build and preview

```bash
node build.mjs
```

The build renders `src/pages/**/*.html` into `dist/` and copies `src/assets`, `robots.txt` and `llms.txt`. Every stylesheet and script link gets `?v=` plus a short hash of the file, so after a change browsers load the new file instead of an old cached copy.

Preview: the `.claude/launch.json` config `marjan-site` serves `website/dist` at http://localhost:4321. You can also run any static server on `dist/`.

## Home page

The home page at `/` is `src/pages/index.html`, the site's only design, with background videos in the hero and a scroll effect in each section (details under "The home page design" below). Each section is its own partial in `src/partials/home/*.html`. It uses Plus Jakarta Sans throughout, with no eyebrows above the section titles. Its sections, top to bottom:

- **Hero:** five journeys with a quote planner. No cards or photo caption; the active tab shows only the 10% background fill. The H1 opens with a small label line, "Travel Agency in [CITY]" (`.hero__kicker`, see the phone notes under "Design notes").
- **Trust strip:** licence, MoRA, years, travellers and Google rating.
- **Introduction:** the entity statement, with the logo mark in a spinning seal (900px and wider).
- **Services:** a numbered index list with a large photo; 10 s auto-advance with a progress line.
- **International tour packages & visit visas** (`home/tour-visas.html`, client priority 1): seven flag tiles (Azerbaijan, Thailand, Indonesia, Malaysia, Dubai, Sri Lanka, Singapore) and an "Any country / Ask on WhatsApp" tile (short enough to stay on one line next to Singapore on phones). Each tile opens a sheet with the tour highlights, the visa, what a package can include, "Get a quote" (closes the sheet, picks the service and notes in the quote form, glides there) and "Ask our team" on WhatsApp, side by side, plus links to the service pages.
- **Visa file processing** (`home/visa-files.html`, priority 2): a navy panel titled "Visa File Processing" with "USA, UK, Canada & Schengen" as a smaller second line (two lines on every screen; from 1024px the description sits to its right, like the other section headings), four tiles (USA, UK, Canada, Schengen) with flags the same size as the tour package tiles; each tile opens a sheet (the Schengen one lists all 29 countries).
- **The sheets** are `<dialog class="cs">` elements right after each section (`cs` = country sheet; `.sheet` and `data-sheet` already belong to the rising page sheet). A bottom sheet on phones, a centred card from 760px; the page behind is locked with `html.no-scroll`; the two buttons stay at the bottom while the details scroll (css "COUNTRIES", main.js "Country sheets"). On desktop every sheet in a section has the same height (`--cs-h` in css: tour sheets 753px, visa sheets 721px, never taller than the screen; re-measure if the copy changes); the country photo (25:9 on desktop, 2:1 on phones, the same on tour and visa sheets: New York, London, Moraine Lake and Paris for the visas) stays at the top and the two buttons at the bottom, and only the details between them scroll: the mouse wheel glides them like the page (same 1.5 s ease-out-expo), and their top and bottom edges fade out while there is more to scroll that way (`.is-scrolled` / `.has-more`, animated through `@property --cs-fade-t/-b`). Phones work the same way (photo and buttons fixed, only the details scroll, with the same fades; a finger scrolls them with the phone's own momentum, like the page); the sheet is as tall as its content, up to the screen less 40px. In the markup the buttons (`.cs__cta`) come after `.cs__body`, and the service links are the last thing inside it. To keep the opening smooth: the dialog itself cannot scroll (`overflow: clip`), nothing has `autofocus` (focusing the close button while the card was still below the screen made the browser scroll to it, so the card jumped), and there is no backdrop blur. On phones the card rises on an iOS-style curve, has a grab handle and no scrollbar, and can be swiped down to close; the header photo starts loading when a finger or the mouse reaches the tile. Flags are self-hosted SVGs from flag-icons 7.2.3 (MIT licence, © Panayiotis Lipiridis) in `assets/img/flags/`.
- **Why choose us:** six reasons around an arch-shaped photo (photo first on phones).
- **Umrah packages:** removed from the home page on 21 Nov (the client felt it made the site look Umrah-only). The Umrah page has its own package section, so the unused cards (`partials/home/packages.html`, their `.pk` styles and scroll effect) were deleted on 22 Nov (audit); they are in the git history as `src/partials/v2/packages.html`. The hero planner and the services card name the same packages and lengths as the Umrah page (Economy, 4-Star, 5-Star & VIP, Family, Group, Ramadan; 7, 10, 14, 15, 21 and 28 days): keep them in step.
- **Destinations:** pick a place from the list (square thumbnails, soft selected card) and the large 16:9 photo changes; fits one screen. The client's lists in their order: Skardu, Naran & Kaghan, Murree, Kashmir, Swat, Gilgit; and Azerbaijan, Thailand, Indonesia, Malaysia, Dubai, Sri Lanka, Singapore. `--n` on each `.dsh` is its number of places, so the list always matches the photo's height on desktop. Skardu and Swat are Magnific photos (23 Nov, user request: the old Skardu photo had a hand in it and the old Swat one was Balakot, not Swat; real photos, not AI): Upper Kachura Lake (Fujifilm; `assets/img/dest/upper-kachura-*`) and the Swat River below snow-capped peaks (Canon; `dest/swat-river-*`), 16:9 at 1600 with a square thumbnail at 192 (`-sq-`); the other places are Unsplash (`{{img}}`).
- **Corporate & domestic tours** (`home/tours.html`, priorities 3 and 4): two cards with the places each goes (corporate: Pakistan and abroad; no "anywhere else" chip, the "Plan my own trip" WhatsApp button covers that; it read "Discuss a custom package" until 23 Nov, shortened on user request), under small text labels with no icons. The title links to the service page and its link covers the whole card (`.tcard__link::after`); two buttons sit above it: "Get a quote" (picks the service in the form) and "Discuss a custom package" (WhatsApp, outline with a black icon), always the same width: side by side when both labels fit (about 1200px and up), otherwise one above the other. No badges on the photos and no green buttons (the user found them off-brand). The cards share the grid rows (CSS subgrid), so title, description, chips and buttons line up across both even when one description is longer; the photo is 2:1 (desktop and phones), the chips are small, and a thin line separates the two groups of chips. Each group sits in a soft panel (white chips, a thin line between the groups) and shows its first three chips (two on phones under 460px, so it stays on one line) and a "+N" chip: the rest are hidden the screen-reader way and counted by a CSS counter (`.chips--more`, needs at least four chips), so adding or removing a place updates the number by itself. "Who it is for": Individuals, Families, Honeymoons, Friends & groups, Companies. On phones and small tablets the cards swipe side by side.
- **Air tickets, hotels, insurance & more** (`home/extras.html`, priority 5): four tiles (one per row with the icon beside the text under 360px, 2 by 2 on other phones and tablets, 4 across on desktop), each asking on WhatsApp with a ready-made message. Each tile stacks icon, title, description and link, and the tiles share grid rows (subgrid) so they line up; the descriptions are kept about the same length (3 lines on phones, 2 on desktop). There are no pages for these yet.
- **How it works:** a travel route with no step numbers; a plane glides along it as you scroll; the three descriptions are the same length. The plane leaves the first stop when the line is 85% down the screen and lands on the last stop when the line's end is 35% from the top (half a screen of scrolling on desktop; the line's length plus half a screen on phones), and it eases after the scroll with a 0.4 s time constant, so a fast flick still reads as a smooth flight (`START`, `END`, `TAU` in `main.js`). The screen height it uses is steady (`screenH` in `main.js`), so the toolbars sliding on a phone don't move the plane. On phones the line runs from the first stop to the last and no further (`main.js` writes the last step's height into `--tail`), and each stop lights up as the plane on screen reaches it. The steps start fading in as soon as the line comes onto the screen.
- **Numbers:** small title on top, figures below, no lines. The + and the star after a figure are icons (`i-stat-plus`, `i-stat-star` in the sprite, `.stat__suf`), not the font's characters: the same height (.46em), centred on the digits and the same gap after every figure. The font's own + sat low and its ★ was much bigger.
- **Reviews:** a carousel of 12 real customer reviews from the client (arrows, swipe, dots), no photos or rating line; cards fade out at both edges.
- **Get a quote:** an enquiry form above the FAQ; it emails the details (FormSubmit) or sends them on WhatsApp, with two regular-size buttons side by side ("Send request", "Via WhatsApp"; a little smaller under 480px so both fit); no "no obligation" line. On phones name + phone (same width) and when + travellers share a row; the fields are picked by class (`field--name`, `field--phone`, `field--when`, `field--count`), not by position, since the hidden form-service inputs come first. Its services follow the client's list (Umrah, international tour, visit visa, visa file processing, corporate, domestic, air tickets, hotel, insurance).
- **FAQ** and a **closing call to action**.

The header (with the services mega menu) and the footer are shared partials. Future variations should start from a copy of this page.

## The home page design

The home page at `/` started (as "version 2") from a copy of version 1, adding background videos in the hero and a scroll effect in each section. Version 1 is gone, so on 22 Nov 2026 its code names were changed from `v2` (user request): `partials/v2/` is now `partials/home/` (the home page sections), `css/v2/` and `js/v2/` are now `css/site/` and `js/site/` (bundled as `site.css` and `site.js`, also used by the service pages), the body class `.v2` is now `.site`, and the `data-v2-*` hooks are now `data-fx-*`. Only the old `/v2/` address keeps the name.
- **Page:** `src/pages/index.html`. Each section is its own partial in `src/partials/home/*.html`.
- **Header:** a white bar with rounded ends (`--r-lg`) floating 26 px below the top on tablets and desktop (10 px on phones), on every screen, white over the hero too, so nothing fades as the page starts to move. It still slides away as you scroll down and back as you scroll up. Styles in `css/site/05-header.css`; `--bar: float` there tells `main.js`. From 640 px up, `site.css` sets `--header-h` to 90 px (14 px more than the 76 px in `styles.css`), so the bar keeps its 64 px height and the hero copy, the pinned services and same-page links start below it. On phones the menu button is round, 5 px inside the bar's end.
- **On phones:** the trust strip is a ticker: one row, each credential with its icon on the left and its text on one line, gliding right to left in an endless loop at a steady 36 px a second (`js/site/12-trust.js` adds a hidden copy of the items so the loop joins up; touching or hovering pauses it). It is one 46 px row, so hero + strip fill one screen with room to spare. The rating reads "4.9 Rating on Google" (no star). Services are separated by a grey line with 20 px more room on each side (no blue line). In the hero planner the last tab has 22 px of room after it, so when Corporate is selected it stays clear of the pinned pause button's fade.
- **Site styles and scripts:** `src/assets/css/site/NN-*.css` and `src/assets/js/site/NN-*.js`. The build joins each folder, in name order, into `/assets/css/site.css` and `/assets/js/site.js`. These load on the home page and the service pages, after `styles.css` and `main.js`.
- **Shared scroll helper:** `js/site/00-core.js` (`window.MFX`). It uses one scroll listener for the page and measures elements only while they are near the screen. Effects work from a steady screen height, `MFX.vh()` (CSS 100lvh, the screen with the phone toolbars away; `MFX.svh()` is 100svh, with them showing), measured from an empty fixed box: `innerHeight` grows and shrinks by up to ~150 px as the toolbars slide, and the effects jumped. It changes only when the screen really does; on touch screens a height change under 160 px at the same width is ignored. Effects animate only transform, opacity, clip-path and filter. Every section shows finished without JavaScript.
- **Hero videos:** the client's own 8-second clip for each journey, in `src/assets/video/hero-<key>-{1080,m}.mp4`, with WebP posters (the first frame). There is no location caption.
  - The clip sets the pace: each journey plays its clip once, and as it ends the next journey cuts in at its first frame, so the five clips run on like one film. The active tab fills in step with the clip.
  - While the slideshow is held (the visitor is using the form, the mouse is over the form, the pause button, the hero partly scrolled away), the current clip loops. A click, key press or swipe still crossfades to the chosen journey.
  - The poster shows at once (it is preloaded). Only the active video loads after the page has loaded; the next journey's poster is fetched straight away and its video once the current one is buffered.
  - Every screen except a phone held upright gets `-1080`: the client's own file as uploaded. Its video track is copied byte for byte (not re-encoded); only the silent audio track is left out and the file index moved to the front, so it starts playing before it has fully downloaded.
  - Phones held upright get `-m`, an upright 608 × 1080 cut that follows the subject of each 2-second shot. A crop can't be made without encoding, so it is made from the originals at their own pixel size and near-lossless quality (SSIM 0.994 against the original). On 24 Nov (user request: a smaller site without losing quality) the five `-m` clips were re-encoded with ffmpeg 9.1 (x264, `-preset veryslow`, High profile, 2 s keyframes as before, index at the front, no audio track): CRF 18 for abroad and visa, CRF 20 for corporate, north and umrah, each the smallest setting whose weakest 5% of frames still score VMAF 95+ against the previous clip (means 96.6–97.9), and checked side by side at the worst frames, 2× zoomed. 13.8 MB → 9.2 MB in all (27–41% smaller per clip); the previous files are in git history. The `-1080` clips were tried too and left as uploaded: x264 at the same quality made them 16–68% larger, so they can't shrink without visible loss.
  - Videos pause off-screen and with the pause button. Save-Data connections get posters only, on the 8 s timer (`data-duration`).
  - Sizes per clip: 1.5–5.3 MB (`-1080`, as uploaded), 1.0–2.6 MB (`-m`).
- **Scrolling past the hero:** everything after the hero is one sheet (`<div class="sheet" data-sheet>` in the page) that rises over it. The hero stays put (`position: sticky`) and sinks back: a little lower, smaller and darker. Once the sheet is halfway up, the introduction on it comes in with the scroll. When the hero is fully covered its video pauses. Built to stay light on phones (a fast flick used to show the hero video in blank gaps, because the phone was too busy to draw the page in time): `js/site/10-hero.js` writes the hero's translate and scale, the darkening layer's opacity (`.hero__dim`, added by the script) and the introduction's opacity and position as inline styles on just those elements (a CSS variable would restyle everything inside them on every frame). The hero is its own layer (`will-change: transform`) and so is the darkening, so nothing is repainted while you scroll; the corners round once (`.is-lifting`). Phones and tablets have no frosted blur on the planner or the WhatsApp button (it was redrawn for every video frame). Once the sheet's top is 40 px above the screen, `body.is-hero-gone` makes the hero see-through and the backdrop page-coloured. The hero's sticky top and the cover use `MFX.svh()`, not `innerHeight` (see the scroll helper), so a phone's toolbars sliding away don't make a hero taller than the screen jump.
- **Services (desktop):** scrolling through the pinned list pushes the photos like a vertical strip. The old photo goes up and out as the next one rises in from the bottom; scrolling back up reverses it. This also runs with reduced motion on, without the zoom. The photo frame has no backdrop of its own, so while a photo loads the section background shows (both versions). The scene keeps one height whichever service is open (as tall as the list with its longest service open, `--svx-min` in `js/site/20-story.js`) and has no scroll anchoring: when it grew and shrank with each service, the browser moved the page, the scroll got stuck flipping between two services (27 Nov, after the client's longer Umrah and holiday texts). Short windows first try tighter rows (`.is-tight`), then fall back to a plain list (`.is-loose`).
- **Services and closing photos:** the client's own photos, self-hosted as WebP in `src/assets/img/services/` and `src/assets/img/cta/`. Each is a `<picture>` with its own crop per layout (art direction), so it fills its frame with nothing important cut off (`object-fit: cover` only trims a few per cent). **One file per crop** (24 Nov, user request: a smaller site): every photo keeps one WebP per ratio, its largest, and the smaller widths of the same crop were deleted (75 files, 4.7 MB; backed up outside the repo); so `srcset`s hold one file, and `{{photo}}` and `home/why` name only the largest. Desktop pages download a little more as a result (measured 24 Nov: up to +0.8 MB of photos on About and Services, +1 MB on the home page); phones with sharp screens already got these files. To bring back smaller sizes, resize from the kept file:
  - Services: phones get a 2:1 crop (`<key>-m-1200`), tablets 4:3 (`<key>-t-840`) and desktop 15:16 (`<key>-1200`, or 900/1140 where the original is smaller), for the frames: 2:1 on phones, 4:3 on tablets, about 0.9–1.0 on desktop from 1280 px wide. Between 1024 and 1279 px the desktop frame narrows to 0.7, so the sides are trimmed; at exactly 1024 px the outer two faces in the corporate photo touch the edges.
  - Closing CTA (`green-canyon-*`): phones 4:5 (sky behind the headline, the boat beside the buttons), tablets 3:2 and desktop 5:2 (the boat whole, on the right, clear of the text). The card's breakpoint is 900 px, so the tablet crop is used up to 899 px.
  - Made from the originals (kept outside the repo) by cropping to lossless PNG first, then WebP quality 80 (78 for the busy Domestic and CTA photos) with ffmpeg/libwebp. Files are 15–260 KB (1x desktops load the 600 px service files, 37–72 KB). `picture` has `display: contents` in these frames, so the `<img>` lays out as before and the scripts and CSS that target `.svx__media img` and `.cta__media img` work unchanged.
- **Hooks in `main.js`:** `main.js` has optional hooks, used by `site.js`: `data-duration` on the hero, `data-caption` on a slide, `data-no-parallax`, and `hero.journeyClock(run)`, which lets the background clip set the pace (it returns 0–1, or `null` for the timer). It also fires a `journey` event on every switch, with `auto: true` when the slideshow moved on by itself.

Video sources: the five hero clips (Umrah, Pakistan tours, holidays abroad, visas, corporate) are the client's own edits, 8 seconds each with a cut every 2 seconds. The originals are kept outside the repo (`Marjan Header Video/Header Video/01–05.mp4`).

## The other service pages, the services index, About us and Contact us

Made on 22 Nov 2026 from the Umrah page's parts, so all inner pages look and scroll the same. Shortcodes:
- `{{> partial}}`
- `{{> partial key="value"}}`: the same, filling the partial's `{{arg key|default}}` slots (the default when a value is not given); used for the country tiles' title and lead
- `{{icon name}}`
- `{{ph KEY}}`: a yellow placeholder
- `{{img id="…" alt="…" ratio="4/5" widths="400,800" sizes="…" [eager] [y=".75" x=".6" zoom="1.2"] [phone="4/3" phone-y=".6" phone-sizes="…"]}}` (`y`/`x`: Unsplash cuts the crop around that point instead of the middle, 0 top/left to 1 bottom/right, and `zoom` zooms in on it, e.g. to leave out something at an edge; `phone`: a separate crop for phones, cut by Unsplash around `phone-y`, 0 top to 1 bottom)
- `{{photo name alt="…" [sizes="…"]}}`: one of the client's own photos in `assets/img/services/` (`name` = umrah, international, visa, corporate, domestic) as a `<picture>` sized for the inner pages’ photo frames: the 4:3 crop on phones, 2:1 on tablets and 15:16 on desktop
- `{{faq-schema folder/faq}}`: the FAQPage JSON-LD built from that FAQ partial's questions and answers (plain text), so the schema always matches the page
- `{{season key}}` / `{{season-text key}}`: a Ramadan, Hajj or Umrah season date that moves on by itself (see "Ramadan and Hajj dates" under Design notes). `{{season}}` is a `<span data-season>` that the browser brings up to date; `{{season-text}}` is plain text for `<title>`, meta, JSON-LD, attributes and `<option>`, fixed at build time.

- Ramadan and Hajj dates (`assets/js/seasons.js`, 22 Nov, user request): nobody types them in.
